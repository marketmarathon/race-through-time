## Video description (draft)
About the 2026–2030 bars (the full wording of the explanation page, IQ-18e): 2026 shows what each company plans to spend, from its results calls and releases; Amazon's and Oracle's plans as reported by Reuters (Oracle's year runs to May 2027); Microsoft's and Meta's plans include leases (Meta: principal payments on finance leases), which their race bars do not. Alibaba: Citi's estimate (year to March 2027). Tencent and Baidu gave no 2026 figure, so their bars show their latest 12 months, to June 2026 (Tencent said it aims to boost capital spending in 2026, Reuters, 18 Mar 2026). ByteDance: a press report from unnamed sources (South China Morning Post, 9 May 2026), not added to the total. 2027–2030 are Race Through Time estimates, not company forecasts: each company's 2026 figure grown at FactSet consensus growth rates (FactSet consensus as of 31 Aug 2026, published by Morgan Stanley Investment Management, 17 Sep 2026); Alibaba to March 2029 uses Citi's estimates. 2030 is the least reliable year; Oracle's 2027–2028 estimates are probably high, because its 2026 figure already runs to May 2027. Both rating agencies expect CoreWeave's spending to dip in 2027 (Fitch, 17 Jul 2026; S&P Global Ratings, 9 Apr 2026); our estimate does not.

What the bars show: each company's capital spending (cash spent on buildings and equipment, as each company reports it) over the previous 12 months, in US dollars, from its own filings. Amazon is shown net of proceeds and incentives; Tencent's figure is measured differently (additions, including some intangible assets); Alibaba's figures before April 2016 include some licensed content and other intangible assets, and its figures for mid-2016 to 2017 are not comparable, so it is not shown then. Chinese companies' figures are converted at the average Federal Reserve H.10 exchange rate for each quarter. The bars are total capital spending, not AI-only spending: no company reports AI spending separately.

Story moments: Microsoft and OpenAI (23 Jan 2023), Microsoft (30 Jul 2024 results call), Stargate (OpenAI, SoftBank, Oracle and MGX, 21 Jan 2025), OpenAI and Oracle (23 Sep 2025), Alphabet (29 Apr 2026 results call) and Alphabet's share sale (Form 8-K, 2 Jun 2026). The OpenAI deals are commitments, not capital spending, and are never added together; the share sale is financing, not capital spending.

2026 plans: Alphabet, Microsoft (about $175bn, including finance leases), Meta (including principal payments on finance leases) and CoreWeave from their results calls and releases; Amazon and Oracle (its fiscal year to May 2027) as reported by Reuters; Alibaba: Citi estimate (fiscal year to March 2027); Tencent and Baidu: actual 12 months to June 2026 (Tencent said it aims to boost capital spending in 2026, Reuters, 18 Mar 2026, with no amount; Baidu gives no outlook). ByteDance: a press report from unnamed sources (South China Morning Post, 9 May 2026) of AI-infrastructure spending of more than 200 billion yuan, not company guidance and not added to the total.

Estimate: iCapital (30 Apr 2026, footnoting S&P Capital IQ) estimates that 70–75% of Amazon's, Microsoft's, Alphabet's and Meta's projected 2026 spending is now tied directly to AI infrastructure. It is never applied to any bar.

Look-ahead 2027–2030: Race Through Time estimates, not forecasts by the companies: each company's 2026 figure grown at FactSet consensus growth rates (FactSet consensus as of 31 Aug 2026, published by Morgan Stanley Investment Management, 17 Sep 2026); Alibaba uses Citi's estimates to March 2029. 2030 is the least reliable year. Oracle's 2027–2028 estimates are probably high. Both rating agencies expect CoreWeave's spending to dip in 2027 (Fitch, 17 Jul 2026; S&P Global Ratings, 9 Apr 2026).

When does it peak? BCG (12 Jun 2026), FactSet consensus (as of 31 Aug 2026, published by Morgan Stanley Investment Management, 17 Sep 2026) and Allianz Research (28 Sep 2026, AI capital spending only) disagree.
